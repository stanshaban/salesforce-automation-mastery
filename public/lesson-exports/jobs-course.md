# Choose a Target Role and Read Job Requirements

JR01 · jobs · About 60 minutes with practice

![Three career paths with one selected route and a small evidence card](https://sam-portal-production.up.railway.app/lesson-images/JR01.jpg)

Turn a vague goal into a shortlist of roles and a skills map you can support with examples.

## Before you begin

- An interest in Salesforce work. A completed exercise helps, but is not required to begin role research.

## What you will learn

- Compare responsibilities before titles.
- Separate requirements from preferences.
- Use evidence to choose a preparation priority.

## 1. A job title is a starting point, not a definition

A Salesforce Developer builds and maintains features on the Salesforce platform. The balance of configuration, Apex, Lightning Web Components, integrations and support varies between teams. A title such as “Senior Salesforce Developer” is therefore not a complete description of the work. Read what the person will actually do and what the employer expects them to know.

For this exercise, imagine that you have completed the Northstar test-drive application. You can explain its data model, write a bulk-safe trigger and build a small LWC. You have not operated a large production integration. These facts are more useful than deciding, without evidence, that you are “middle level.” They tell you which requirements you can demonstrate and which require preparation.

If you have not completed a project yet, leave the evidence column empty or write “not yet demonstrated.” Use the fictional Northstar example to understand the method, then choose a first learning task. You can research a target before you are ready to apply; do not copy the example as your own experience.

> **💡 Key idea**
>
> Choose roles by responsibilities and evidence, not by the title alone.

## 2. Separate three kinds of requirement

A practical requirement affects whether the role can work for you: location, time zone, working arrangement or a stated employment condition. A technical requirement describes work you must be able to perform, such as maintaining Apex integrations. A preference may improve your fit without being essential, such as previous experience in the automotive industry. Use the employer’s wording; do not turn every item into a mandatory rule.

Take this fictional description: “Maintain Apex and LWC features, support REST integrations, work with a UK-based team; Automotive Cloud experience preferred.” Your Northstar LWC is relevant evidence. A completed mock callout exercise shows practice, but it does not prove experience maintaining a production integration. The industry preference is a separate gap. “UK-based team” does not by itself establish where the employer can hire; that still needs clarification.



## 3. Build a small evidence matrix

Create a table with the columns requirement, employer wording, importance, evidence and next step. Mark evidence as “I can demonstrate this,” “I have practised this” or “I have not used this.” These labels prevent a tutorial from silently turning into commercial experience. Attach a concrete item: a repository, a test result, an explanation or an authorised work example.

Start with five genuine job descriptions, keeping a direct link and the date you reviewed each one. Highlight repeated responsibilities rather than counting every keyword equally. If four roles require integration support and your only gap is integration diagnosis, that is a useful study signal. If every role is outside your preferred working arrangement, changing your technical study plan will not solve the practical mismatch.

> **⚡ Try it**
>
> Collect five descriptions before changing your CV. First find the work you are trying to be hired to do.

## 4. Choose a primary and an adjacent target

Select one primary role whose central responsibilities you can credibly discuss. Choose one adjacent role only if you can explain the difference in preparation. For example, your primary target might be a developer role focused on Apex and LWC; an adjacent target might add integration ownership. Do not describe the second role as equivalent if its responsibilities are substantially broader.

Write a three-sentence target statement: the work you want, the evidence you bring and the area you are developing. “I want to build Salesforce service applications. My strongest examples are a tested test-drive feature and an accessible LWC. I am extending that work with integration failure handling.” This is an internal planning tool, not a claim that you are guaranteed to qualify. Review it when you have better evidence from actual applications.

> **🔎 Check your understanding**
>
> Another person should be able to connect every claimed strength to one example in your matrix.

## ⚡ Independent practice: Apply this to your own search

Choose a realistic target using five current job descriptions.

1. Save five employer descriptions with links and review dates.
2. Separate practical conditions, technical responsibilities and preferences.
3. Map each important requirement to something you can demonstrate, have practised or have not used.
4. Write a primary target, one adjacent target and a short preparation priority.

### Your work is ready when

- No requirement is marked essential unless the description supports that interpretation.
- Tutorial work is identified as practice rather than employment.
- The chosen target is explained with evidence, not only a desired title.

## 🔎 Check your understanding

Answer in your own words before reading the explanations.

### 1. Does a senior title prove that two roles require the same skills?

**Answer:** No. Responsibilities, context and expected ownership may differ. Compare the descriptions.

### 2. Is a missing preferred skill an automatic rejection?

**Answer:** You cannot infer that. Keep it distinct from stated requirements and decide whether the core work fits.

### 3. What makes a skills gap actionable?

**Answer:** A specific requirement, your current evidence and a concrete next exercise or question to resolve it.

## What to take away

- Compare responsibilities before titles.
- Separate requirements from preferences.
- Use evidence to choose a preparation priority.

## Sources and further reading

- [Salesforce career paths](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/career-path)

# Use AI to Research and Verify Job Opportunities

JR02 · jobs · About 60 minutes with practice

![A magnifying glass checking one job card while duplicate cards sit aside](https://sam-portal-production.up.railway.app/lesson-images/JR02.jpg)

Use an AI assistant to organise job research while checking every opportunity against a real employer source.

## Before you begin

- JR01: a target role and an evidence matrix.

## What you will learn

- Bound the research request.
- Verify the employer source.
- Keep facts, uncertainty and decisions separate.

## 1. Give the assistant a bounded research task

An AI assistant can help turn a broad search into structured work. It can suggest search terms, compare descriptions and extract responsibilities. Whether it can find current vacancies depends on the tools available in that session. A model that cannot access current pages must not be treated as a live job feed.

Start with the target statement from JR01. Add the locations or working arrangements you are willing to consider, the type of role and any explicit exclusions. Keep sensitive personal details out of the research prompt. The assistant does not need your full address, passport or private employment documents to help compare public descriptions. Ask it to distinguish observed facts from questions that remain unanswered.

> **⚠️ Watch out**
>
> A confident-looking vacancy can still be invented, stale or incorrectly interpreted.

## 2. Search with evidence fields

Ask for a small batch rather than hundreds of results. For each candidate, request the employer, exact role title, direct source URL, visible location, stated remote conditions, date checked and the specific responsibility that matches your target. If the page does not state a condition, the table should say “Not stated.”

Suppose a result says “Remote Salesforce Developer — Europe.” Before treating that as a match, open the employer page. The actual description may restrict hiring to a short list of countries or require regular office visits. The assistant’s summary is useful only when it preserves those limits. A search result snippet or an aggregator’s copied title is not enough to settle eligibility.

> **⚡ Try it**
>
> Open the employer’s vacancy page yourself before adding a role to your active application queue.

**Reusable working prompt or template**

```text
Research Salesforce Developer opportunities matching this target: [target].
Use current sources only if browsing is available. If it is not, say so.
For each result return: employer, exact title, direct employer URL,
location, stated remote conditions, date checked and matching responsibilities.
Separate observed facts from unresolved questions. Do not invent missing fields.
Return a maximum of five candidates. Do not apply or contact anyone.
```

## 3. Check the original page and remove duplicates

Use a verification pass with four questions. Does the page exist? Does it still present an open application route? Does the description match the summary? Does the employer identity match the domain and application destination? A page can remain visible after a role closes, so a URL alone is not proof that applications are still accepted.

Duplicates often appear under several agencies, job boards or slightly different titles. Compare employer, location, responsibilities and any job reference number. Keep one primary record and add secondary links only if they help. Do not merge two different roles simply because their titles match. Record the reason for a duplicate decision so you can reverse it when new information appears.



## 4. Turn research into a decision

Give each verified result one next step: apply, investigate a missing condition, save for later or skip. Write a short reason. “Apply: the role mainly uses Apex and LWC, and I have two relevant examples” is better than an unsupported percentage fit score. “Investigate: remote hiring countries are not stated” preserves uncertainty instead of hiding it.

AI can draft a comparison, but you decide whether the factual evidence supports it. Review one result that you rejected as well as one you accepted. This catches overly narrow filters and makes the process useful even when the first search produces few matches. Keep the date of verification; current vacancies can change after the lesson.

> **🔎 Check your understanding**
>
> Every role you plan to act on has an original source, a review date and a reasoned next step.

## ⚡ Independent practice: Apply this to your own search

Research a small batch of roles and verify the strongest candidates.

1. Use the prompt with your target role and actual constraints.
2. Verify five proposed opportunities against original employer pages.
3. Correct unsupported fields and merge genuine duplicates.
4. Choose an action and reason for each verified result.

### Your work is ready when

- No role is accepted solely on an AI-generated link or summary.
- Unknown conditions remain explicitly unknown.
- The list includes a date checked and a direct source for each active opportunity.

## 🔎 Check your understanding

Answer in your own words before reading the explanations.

### 1. What if the assistant cannot browse?

**Answer:** Use it to generate search terms or compare descriptions you provide. Do not ask it to pretend it has live vacancies.

### 2. Why is a relevance percentage weak evidence?

**Answer:** It can conceal assumptions. A requirement-to-evidence explanation shows what actually matches.

### 3. What should happen when remote conditions are unclear?

**Answer:** Mark them as unstated and verify them through the employer’s published process before assuming eligibility.

## What to take away

- Bound the research request.
- Verify the employer source.
- Keep facts, uncertainty and decisions separate.

## Sources and further reading

- [Salesforce career paths](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/career-path)

# Build a Repeatable Job Search Workflow

JR03 · jobs · About 60 minutes with practice

![A calm task board with one job card moving toward a calendar tile](https://sam-portal-production.up.railway.app/lesson-images/JR03.jpg)

Create a small system that makes the next useful action visible without turning job hunting into administration.

## Before you begin

- JR01 and JR02: a target role and verified opportunities.

## What you will learn

- Make stage transitions observable.
- Automate collection and reminders carefully.
- Review next actions and patterns, not only application counts.

## 1. Track decisions, not just links

A folder of bookmarks is a collection, not a workflow. A useful job-search record tells you what the opportunity is, what you know about it and what to do next. You can use a spreadsheet, a simple board or another tool you already understand. No custom application is required for this lesson.

Keep the fields small: employer, role, source URL, date checked, stage, next action, next-action date and notes. Add a link to the CV version only after applying. The purpose is to prevent an opportunity from disappearing between “I found this” and “I decided what to do.” You do not need a complicated score or twenty pipeline stages.

> **💡 Key idea**
>
> A useful record has a next action. A stage alone does not tell you what to do.

## 2. Define stages you can apply consistently

Use stages such as Found, Verified, Preparing, Applied, Interview and Closed. Define what moves a record forward. Found means you have a candidate link. Verified means the original description has been checked. Applied means you have actually submitted through the chosen application route, not merely generated a draft.

For a fictional Northstar developer vacancy, the next action might be “Read the integration responsibilities and choose a portfolio example.” After that, it might be “Tailor the summary and review the final CV.” Avoid moving a record to Applied because you planned to apply. That small distinction makes your later review of the search much more accurate.



## 3. Use alerts as input, not as decisions

If your chosen job service offers saved searches or alerts, create one for your primary role and one for a useful adjacent role. Use the service’s current settings and check the result before relying on the alert. Notifications can contain duplicates, old listings or roles outside your constraints; send them through the same verification process as manual searches.

Automation can collect a link or remind you to review it. It should not silently decide that a role is suitable or submit an application on your behalf. If you later build a personal automation, keep a visible review step before anything is sent. In this lesson, a recurring calendar reminder and a saved search are sufficient.

> **⚡ Try it**
>
> Test the workflow with one sample record before adding a large backlog.

## 4. Review the process weekly

Choose a realistic review rhythm, for example two short research sessions and one weekly review. At the review, look for stalled next actions, descriptions that have closed and preparation work that is consuming time without helping applications. Move closed records out of the active view rather than deleting the history you need for learning.

Use counts as observations, not promises: verified opportunities, completed applications, interview invitations and repeated reasons for a mismatch. Small samples are noisy. If a few applications receive no response, you cannot conclude that a particular keyword caused it. Look for repeated patterns and change one part of the process deliberately, such as targeting more closely matched roles or making your project description clearer.

> **🔎 Check your understanding**
>
> You can explain which records need action this week and why. You do not need to reconstruct the answer from memory.

## ⚡ Independent practice: Apply this to your own search

Build and run a small job-search board for one week.

1. Create the eight core fields and define six stages.
2. Add three verified roles and one deliberately incomplete sample.
3. Give each active record a next action and date.
4. Create a review reminder and test one saved search if your service supports it.
5. At the end of the week, record one process improvement and its reason.

### Your work is ready when

- Applied means actually submitted.
- Unverified opportunities cannot silently enter the application queue.
- Every active record has a next step; closed records retain their useful history.

## 🔎 Check your understanding

Answer in your own words before reading the explanations.

### 1. Why not create dozens of stages?

**Answer:** Extra stages add work unless they change a decision. Start with a few observable transitions.

### 2. Does an alert prove a job is suitable?

**Answer:** No. It is an input to verification, not a recommendation you must accept.

### 3. What can you infer from two rejected applications?

**Answer:** Very little about cause. Record observations and look for broader patterns before making confident conclusions.

## What to take away

- Make stage transitions observable.
- Automate collection and reminders carefully.
- Review next actions and patterns, not only application counts.

## Sources and further reading

- [Salesforce career paths](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/career-path)

# Describe Your Experience with Evidence

JR04 · jobs · About 60 minutes with practice

![Three concrete project artifacts connected to one personal contribution card](https://sam-portal-production.up.railway.app/lesson-images/JR04.jpg)

Explain what you built, why it mattered and what you can actually prove.

## Before you begin

- JR01: a target role.
- At least one piece of work you have actually completed.

## What you will learn

- Describe your own contribution.
- Connect results to evidence.
- Keep the scope of measurements clear.

## 1. Start with a specific piece of work

An experience statement is useful when another person can understand your responsibility. “Worked with Salesforce” names a platform but says little about your work. “Built a test-drive request form that validates the vehicle and records the customer’s chosen time” describes a feature someone can discuss with you.

Use three kinds of evidence carefully. Commercial work comes from a real employment or client context. A personal project is work you designed and completed yourself. A guided exercise follows a learning path. All three can demonstrate knowledge, but they are different contexts. Label the Northstar project as a training project unless you have separately delivered it for a real business.

> **💡 Key idea**
>
> A precise training example is more useful than an impressive claim that you cannot explain. It also gives the interviewer a clear starting point.

## 2. Build a short explanation from five parts

Write the situation, the requirement, your contribution, the result and how you checked it. For Northstar, the situation is that salespeople need a consistent way to request test drives. The requirement is to capture a customer, an available vehicle and a time. Your contribution might be the validation logic and its tests. The result is an implemented feature. Your evidence is a repeatable test and a short demonstration.

Be exact about ownership. If another learner designed the data model and you wrote the LWC, say that. If you extended a supplied starter class, identify the extension. This does not weaken the account; it shows that you understand the boundary of your responsibility. Avoid describing every part of a team project as something you personally delivered.



**Reusable working prompt or template**

```text
Context: [the process or problem]
Requirement: [the behaviour needed]
My contribution: [what I personally changed]
Result: [what happened, with evidence]
Verification: [how I checked it]
Limitations: [what I did not implement or measure]
```

## 3. Use measurements only when you have them

A result does not have to contain a percentage. “Handles a test batch of 200 records without queries inside the loop” is a concrete technical result if you ran that test. “Improved sales productivity by 40%” is not supported merely because your form takes fewer clicks in a demonstration. Business impact needs a suitable baseline, a measurement period and a way to connect the change to the observation.

If you have a measurement, keep its scope. A local test that falls from 800 milliseconds to 300 milliseconds does not prove that production response times improved by the same amount. Explain the environment, the sample and the limitation. If you did not measure an outcome, describe what the feature enables and how you verified its behaviour instead of inventing a metric.

> **⚠️ Watch out**
>
> Do not turn a test result, estimate or expected benefit into a measured commercial outcome.

## 4. Prepare a short and a detailed version

Write a three-sentence version for a CV or introduction, then a two-minute version for an interview. The short version should name the feature, your work and the evidence. The longer version should add one difficulty, one decision and one limitation. Practice both without reading the text word for word.

For example: “I built a Salesforce training application for test-drive requests. I implemented bulk-safe validation and tests for unavailable vehicles and mixed valid and invalid records. I can demonstrate the behaviour in a development org; I have not deployed it for a real dealership.” That explanation gives the interviewer useful questions to ask without creating a false impression of production experience.

> **🔎 Check your understanding**
>
> Ask a peer to identify your contribution and your evidence after hearing the explanation once. If they cannot, simplify it.

## ⚡ Independent practice: Apply this to your own search

Write two evidence-based accounts: one completed feature and one problem you investigated.

1. Identify the context and your exact contribution.
2. Collect an authorised code example, test result or demonstration.
3. Write a three-sentence summary and a two-minute account.
4. Underline every measurement and check its source.
5. Add one limitation that you can discuss honestly.

### Your work is ready when

- The reader can distinguish your work from the team’s or starter project’s work.
- Every number has a measurement source and scope.
- Training work is labelled and still explains a meaningful technical result.

## 🔎 Check your understanding

Answer in your own words before reading the explanations.

### 1. Must every CV achievement include a percentage?

**Answer:** No. A verified capability or technical result is useful; an invented percentage is misleading.

### 2. How should you describe a tutorial extension?

**Answer:** Name the guided starting point and explain the part you changed independently.

### 3. Why include a limitation?

**Answer:** It makes the boundary of your evidence clear and creates a realistic technical discussion.

## What to take away

- Describe your own contribution.
- Connect results to evidence.
- Keep the scope of measurements clear.

## Sources and further reading

- [Salesforce career paths](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/career-path)

# Write a Clear and Honest Salesforce CV

JR05 · jobs · About 60 minutes with practice

![A clean resume page linked to a small tested project and a factual evidence card](https://sam-portal-production.up.railway.app/lesson-images/JR05.jpg)

Create a readable CV that connects your skills to relevant work without inventing experience.

## Before you begin

- JR01 and JR04: a target role and evidence-based experience accounts.

## What you will learn

- Use a readable structure.
- Support skills with examples.
- Inspect the final exported file.

## 1. Give the reader a clear route through your experience

A CV helps an employer decide what to ask you about. It should make your target role, relevant experience and strongest evidence easy to find. Start with your name and contact information, a short role-focused summary, experience or projects, skills and education or certifications where relevant. The order can change with your background; a career changer may place relevant projects before unrelated employment.

Use ordinary section names and readable formatting. Keep your contact details as text, and check that the exported file preserves selectable text. There is no universal layout that guarantees success in every applicant tracking system. A simple document is a practical choice because both people and software need to identify its information.

> **💡 Key idea**
>
> The CV is an invitation to a detailed conversation. Every important claim should lead to an example you can explain.

## 2. Write the summary after collecting evidence

Use the target from JR01 and the evidence from JR04. A useful summary might say: “Developer building Salesforce applications with Apex and Lightning Web Components. Completed a test-drive training application with validation, automated tests and a documented review workflow. Seeking a role focused on application development and learning integration support.” Adjust this to your actual background rather than copying it as a personal claim.

Avoid broad labels such as “expert in all Salesforce technologies.” They do not help a reader assess your depth and are difficult to defend. A summary is usually a few focused lines, not a biography or a long list of personality traits. Save the detail for the work examples below it.



## 3. Connect skills to work examples

List skills at the depth you can support. If Apex appears in your skills section, a project or experience entry should show where you used it. Distinguish hands-on practice from production responsibility. For a training project, include the project name, its learning context, the problem, your implementation and a repository or demonstration link when it is safe to share.

Use a consistent bullet pattern: action, object and evidence. “Implemented validation for unavailable vehicles and added tests for mixed-record updates” is clearer than “Responsible for high-quality innovative solutions.” Include dates honestly and identify certification status accurately. A planned exam is not a completed credential. You do not need to hide a career change; explain the relevant transferable work and the Salesforce work you have completed.

> **⚡ Try it**
>
> Draft three project or experience bullets before polishing the visual layout. If the evidence is weak, formatting will not fix it.

## 4. Review the exported document as a reader

Export the CV in the format requested by the employer. Open that exact file, select its text and copy a paragraph into a plain text editor. Check the reading order, missing characters, broken links and accidental truncation. Verify that the file name is descriptive and that the contact details are current. This is a simple quality check, not proof of compatibility with every hiring system.

Ask another person to answer three questions after a brief read: what role are you targeting, what have you actually built, and which example should they ask about first? If the answers are unclear, revise the content. Keep a master CV with your full evidence so that later tailoring changes emphasis rather than rewriting your history.

> **🔎 Check your understanding**
>
> Open the final attachment, not only the editable source. A good draft can still become a broken export.

## ⚡ Independent practice: Apply this to your own search

Produce a master CV based only on experience and projects you can substantiate.

1. Choose a simple readable structure.
2. Write a short target-role summary.
3. Add evidence-based experience or project bullets.
4. Check skills and credential claims against your records.
5. Export and inspect the exact file you would submit.

### Your work is ready when

- The target role and two relevant examples are easy to find.
- No training project is presented as commercial employment.
- The exported file has readable text, working links and current contact details.

## 🔎 Check your understanding

Answer in your own words before reading the explanations.

### 1. Should you claim expertise in a tool you used once?

**Answer:** No. Describe the task you practised and the depth you can demonstrate.

### 2. Can one CV layout guarantee an interview?

**Answer:** No. Layout supports readability; suitability and hiring decisions involve more than formatting.

### 3. When should you list a certification as completed?

**Answer:** Only after earning it. Planned or in-progress study must be labelled separately.

## What to take away

- Use a readable structure.
- Support skills with examples.
- Inspect the final exported file.

## Sources and further reading

- [Career development: land your next opportunity](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/career-development-planning/land-your-next-opportunity)

# Tailor Your CV to a Specific Role

JR06 · jobs · About 60 minutes with practice

![A job description and a resume joined by a few clear evidence-based connections](https://sam-portal-production.up.railway.app/lesson-images/JR06.jpg)

Change emphasis for a vacancy while keeping your experience accurate and consistent.

## Before you begin

- JR05: a master CV.
- One current verified job description.

## What you will learn

- Map requirements to evidence.
- Review AI edits for factual changes.
- Keep a record of the submitted version.

## 1. Tailoring starts with the job, not with synonyms

Tailoring means helping a reader see the relevant parts of your existing experience. It does not mean rewriting your history to match every requirement. Begin with one verified job description and your master CV. Extract the three or four responsibilities that appear central to the role. Keep practical conditions, essential technical requirements and preferences distinct.

Imagine a role focused on Apex integrations and LWC maintenance. Your master CV contains a test-drive form, a mock callout exercise and unrelated earlier software work. The form and callout deserve more space than an unrelated visual design experiment. You still need to label the callout as a training exercise if that is what it was.

> **💡 Key idea**
>
> Tailoring changes selection, order and explanation. It should not change the facts.

## 2. Map each priority to the strongest available evidence

Create a small table: employer responsibility, your evidence, CV location and unresolved gap. For “maintain LWC,” point to a component where you can explain data flow, loading and error states. For “support production integrations,” a mock exercise is partial preparation, not proof that you have owned production incidents. Keep that gap visible in your preparation notes.

Prefer an exact technical term when it accurately describes your work. If you used Queueable Apex, name it instead of saying only “background work.” Do not insert a technology merely because the description contains it. A keyword without a supporting example can create an interview question that exposes the mismatch.



## 3. Use AI as an editor with a boundary

An assistant can compare two supplied documents and propose clearer wording. Tell it to preserve employers, dates, ownership, technologies and measurements. Ask for changes with reasons so that you can review them. If it suggests a new accomplishment, reject the suggestion unless you have independent evidence for it.

Review the output line by line against the master CV. Pay particular attention to subtle upgrades: “practised” becoming “led,” “training org” disappearing, or “supported a developer” becoming “architected the solution.” These changes may sound polished but alter the meaning. The final file is your representation of your experience, so you must understand and approve every claim.

> **⚠️ Watch out**
>
> A more confident verb can change a factual claim. Check ownership and context, not just spelling.

**Reusable working prompt or template**

```text
Compare this job description with my master CV.
Identify the three most relevant responsibilities and the existing evidence for each.
Suggest edits to emphasis and clarity only.
Do not add employers, dates, technologies, metrics, credentials or responsibilities.
Keep training projects labelled as training.
For each proposed edit, explain the reason and quote the evidence it uses.
List unsupported requirements separately as preparation gaps.
```

## 4. Save a version you can reconstruct

Save the tailored CV with the employer, role and date in your private application records. Record the source job description and the actual submitted file. You may later need to explain a project that was highlighted in that version. Keeping the file prevents confusion between several similar applications.

Finish with a consistency check: dates match the master, links work, the summary fits the role and no essential context has vanished. Then decide whether the application is worthwhile given the remaining gaps. A tailored CV can make a real match clearer; it cannot create experience that you do not yet have.

> **🔎 Check your understanding**
>
> For each changed bullet, identify the original evidence and explain why this employer is likely to care about it.

## ⚡ Independent practice: Apply this to your own search

Tailor your master CV to one verified vacancy and keep a change log.

1. Extract the central responsibilities.
2. Map each to evidence and identify gaps.
3. Reorder or clarify the relevant sections.
4. Review every proposed AI edit if you use an assistant.
5. Save the final version with the job description and a short change log.

### Your work is ready when

- Every claim remains consistent with the master CV.
- The most relevant evidence is prominent.
- Unsupported requirements are recorded as gaps rather than disguised as experience.

## 🔎 Check your understanding

Answer in your own words before reading the explanations.

### 1. What should change when tailoring a CV?

**Answer:** Selection, order and wording of relevant evidence, while the facts remain consistent.

### 2. Should all missing keywords be inserted?

**Answer:** No. Use a term only if it accurately describes experience you can explain.

### 3. Why keep the submitted version?

**Answer:** It lets you prepare for the exact examples and claims the interviewer has read.

## What to take away

- Map requirements to evidence.
- Review AI edits for factual changes.
- Keep a record of the submitted version.

## Sources and further reading

- [Salesforce career paths](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/career-path)

# Prepare a Salesforce Portfolio for Review

JR07 · jobs · About 60 minutes with practice

![A small project display with an application, a simple architecture diagram and test evidence](https://sam-portal-production.up.railway.app/lesson-images/JR07.jpg)

Make one project easy for another developer to understand, run and assess.

## Before you begin

- A completed Salesforce exercise or project.
- Basic Git and repository skills from the Junior path.

## What you will learn

- Explain the project before the folders.
- Make setup and verification repeatable.
- Demonstrate a decision and its limits.

## 1. A portfolio is a reviewable explanation

A repository full of metadata is not yet an effective portfolio. A reviewer needs to know what the project does, which part you built, how to try it and what its limitations are. Start with one project you understand well. The Northstar test-drive application is suitable because it connects a visible user workflow to data, automation and tests.

Label it as a training project and describe its fictional business context. Explain who uses the feature: a salesperson records a customer’s test-drive request. The customer is represented by a Contact record and does not automatically have a Salesforce login. This small detail demonstrates that you understand the user and data model rather than only the code.

> **💡 Key idea**
>
> Make the reviewer’s first five minutes useful: purpose, demonstration, setup and evidence.

## 2. Write a README that answers practical questions

Begin with a short problem statement and a screenshot or brief recorded demonstration. Then explain the main objects and the flow of one successful request. Add prerequisites, installation commands that you have actually checked, the test command and the expected result. If a feature requires an Agentforce-enabled org or a separate product, say so before the setup steps.

Include a short design decision section. For example, explain why Test_Drive__c uses lookups and how that affects its access model. A decision is stronger when it includes a trade-off, not just a claim of best practice. Add known limitations such as mock availability or the absence of a real calendar integration. These help a reviewer distinguish the implemented scope from future ideas.



**Reusable working prompt or template**

```text
# Northstar Test-Drive Training Project

## Problem and users
## Short demonstration
## What I implemented
## Data model and request flow
## Prerequisites and setup
## Tests and observed results
## Design decisions
## Known limitations
## Next improvements
```

## 3. Keep the repository safe and reproducible

Before making a project public, inspect the files you are about to publish. Exclude authentication state, private keys, local configuration containing secrets and real customer data. Use synthetic records for demonstrations. The SAM starter already excludes common Salesforce authentication directories, but an ignore file cannot remove a secret that was previously committed.

Use a separate development org and the repository instructions to rehearse setup. If you cannot test a step, label it unverified and explain the dependency. A screenshot of a successful interface is useful, but it does not prove that a clean checkout can reproduce the project. Capture the actual test output and the commit it belongs to, without including credentials.

> **⚠️ Watch out**
>
> A public portfolio should contain work you are authorised to share and synthetic data. Do not copy private employer code into it.

## 4. Guide the reviewer through one important decision

Prepare a two-minute walkthrough: the user problem, the visible feature, one key implementation decision and the verification. Open the relevant code instead of browsing through every folder. Show a failure case as well as a successful request. For example, an unavailable vehicle should produce a useful error rather than a misleading success message.

Ask a peer to follow the README from a clean starting point. Record where they hesitate and improve those instructions. The aim is not to remove every question; good projects invite technical discussion. The aim is to make that discussion about your decisions and evidence rather than missing setup details.

> **🔎 Check your understanding**
>
> A reviewer can identify the project’s context, reproduce a supported path and find the tests without asking you where to start.

## ⚡ Independent practice: Apply this to your own search

Prepare one portfolio repository and rehearse a short review walkthrough.

1. Write the README using the structure above.
2. Add a small diagram and a synthetic-data demonstration.
3. Review publishable files and repository history for private material.
4. Rehearse setup in a suitable development environment.
5. Ask a peer to review the README and record one improvement.

### Your work is ready when

- Training context, ownership and limitations are explicit.
- Setup and test instructions match the project.
- The demonstration includes a success and a failure case.

## 🔎 Check your understanding

Answer in your own words before reading the explanations.

### 1. Does a screenshot prove reproducibility?

**Answer:** No. It shows one observed state. Setup instructions and repeatable tests provide different evidence.

### 2. What makes a design decision useful to a reviewer?

**Answer:** The requirement, chosen approach, alternative and trade-off are clear.

### 3. Why is one well-explained project often enough to begin?

**Answer:** It gives concrete material for a technical conversation; adding unexplained repositories does not automatically add evidence.

## What to take away

- Explain the project before the folders.
- Make setup and verification repeatable.
- Demonstrate a decision and its limits.

# Practise a Technical Interview with AI

JR08 · jobs · About 60 minutes with practice

![Two simply drawn people discussing one technical diagram with a reflective note card](https://sam-portal-production.up.railway.app/lesson-images/JR08.jpg)

Run a structured mock interview that reveals gaps instead of rewarding memorised wording.

## Before you begin

- At least one completed technical module and one project example.

## What you will learn

- Bound the mock interview.
- Verify factual feedback.
- Practise changed conditions.

## 1. Choose a narrow practice goal

A useful mock interview has a scope. “Ask me Salesforce questions” can produce a random mixture of definitions, advanced features and outdated assumptions. Choose a level, two topics and a time limit. For example, a Junior session could cover objects, relationships and simple Apex tests. A Middle session could cover a callout failure and LWC state. A Senior session could examine access changes during an integration redesign.

Tell the assistant which parts of the Northstar project you have completed. Ask it to question your decisions rather than inventing employment history for you. It should ask one question at a time, wait for your answer and use a follow-up when your explanation is vague. Keep the first session short enough that you can review it properly.

> **💡 Key idea**
>
> The value of a mock interview is the next useful practice task, not a flattering score.

## 2. Set rules for evidence and feedback

An AI interviewer can be mistaken, especially about changing platform behaviour and product availability. Ask it to separate factual feedback from communication feedback. A factual correction should include a current official source that you can verify. “Your answer was difficult to follow” is communication feedback and needs an example from your response rather than a documentation link.

Use a small rubric: technical correctness, reasoning, evidence and clarity. Score each as missing, partial or clear. If you say “use asynchronous Apex,” the follow-up should ask which mechanism, why it fits and what happens on failure. This tests understanding more effectively than giving full credit for a keyword.



**Reusable working prompt or template**

```text
Act as a practice interviewer for [level] Salesforce development.
Topics: [two topics]. Project context: [what I actually built].
Ask one question at a time and wait for my answer.
Use a follow-up when I make an unsupported claim. Do not reveal the answer first.
After five questions, review correctness, reasoning, evidence and clarity.
Quote the specific part of my answer behind each observation.
For platform corrections, provide an official source and identify version assumptions.
Mark uncertainty. Do not invent a hiring result or guarantee my level.
```

## 3. Answer with a decision and a reason

Use a simple response structure: state the relevant fact or decision, explain why, give an example and mention an important boundary. For “Why use a lookup here?”, you might explain that the test-drive record has its own ownership and sharing requirements, show the model and discuss what a master-detail alternative would change. Do not force every answer into a long speech; the structure is a guide, not a script.

When you do not know, identify the missing fact and explain how you would verify it. “I would check the current limit and the expected volume before choosing the batch size” is useful if you can also explain the design issue. Avoid using uncertainty as a way to say nothing: give the part you understand, then mark the boundary.

> **⚡ Try it**
>
> Record your answer before reading the model explanation. Otherwise you may mistake recognition for recall.

## 4. Review the transcript and retry one problem

After the session, choose two weak answers and verify the assistant’s feedback against official documentation or your own reproducible test. Keep a table with question, your original answer, confirmed issue and next exercise. Reject a correction if its evidence does not support it; the assistant is a practice partner, not the final authority.

Repeat a related question with a changed condition. If the first question used a single record, ask about a batch. If it assumed private sharing, ask what changes with another access path. You should be able to explain the changed reasoning. Repeating the exact wording until it sounds fluent is not enough to establish understanding.

> **🔎 Check your understanding**
>
> You can explain one correction, cite or demonstrate why it is correct and answer a variation without reading your notes.

## ⚡ Independent practice: Apply this to your own search

Run a five-question mock interview and turn it into a small practice plan.

1. Choose a level and two topics.
2. Use the prompt and answer before viewing feedback.
3. Verify two factual corrections.
4. Rewrite the weakest answer in your own words.
5. Answer a variation that changes an important condition.

### Your work is ready when

- The session contains follow-up questions, not only definitions.
- Corrections are supported or explicitly marked uncertain.
- The final plan names a concrete exercise rather than “study more.”

## 🔎 Check your understanding

Answer in your own words before reading the explanations.

### 1. Is an AI interview score a reliable hiring prediction?

**Answer:** No. It is practice feedback and can be inconsistent or wrong.

### 2. How can you check whether you understand a corrected answer?

**Answer:** Explain the mechanism and solve a variation with changed conditions.

### 3. What should you do with an unsupported correction?

**Answer:** Verify it using official documentation or a suitable experiment before accepting it.

## What to take away

- Bound the mock interview.
- Verify factual feedback.
- Practise changed conditions.

## Sources and further reading

- [Get job ready](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/trails/get-job-ready)

# Practise Live Coding and Code Review

JR09 · jobs · About 60 minutes with practice

![A code card with one highlighted flaw beside a small timer and test cases](https://sam-portal-production.up.railway.app/lesson-images/JR09.jpg)

Show your reasoning while solving a small problem and reviewing a flawed implementation.

## Before you begin

- Junior Apex collections and testing lessons, or equivalent skills.
- A separate training org with starter/salesforce deployed and access to create Apex classes and run tests.
- The starter Test_Drive__c object and its Vehicle__c lookup, plus Vehicle__c records created inside each test fixture.

## What you will learn

- Clarify the contract.
- Test examples before polishing.
- Review impact before style.

## 1. Clarify the task before typing

Live coding combines implementation with communication. Begin by restating the requirement and asking about ambiguous inputs, expected output and constraints. For a practice task, use this request: “Given a list of test-drive records, return the number of requests for each vehicle.” Clarify whether records without a vehicle should be ignored and whether the method only reads the supplied records.

For this lesson, ignore null vehicle references and do not query or update the database. The input list contains the data you need. This boundary makes the exercise small enough to discuss. If an interviewer adds database access or security requirements, treat that as a changed task and explain what additional decisions it creates.

Use the actual starter API names: Test_Drive__c is the appointment object and its Vehicle__c field is a lookup. Implement countByVehicle(List<Test_Drive__c> requests) returning Map<Id, Integer> in your own class. The starter does not include that class or its tests. Keep each class metadata file at the course API 68.0 baseline. If no suitable org is available, write the algorithm and cases, but mark compilation and Apex execution as outstanding.

> **💡 Key idea**
>
> A small clarification can prevent you from implementing the wrong problem very efficiently.

## 2. Sketch the algorithm and examples

Use a map from vehicle ID to count. For each record with a vehicle, read the current count or use zero, add one and store the result. Walk through three records: two for vehicle A and one for vehicle B. The result should contain A with two and B with one. An empty list should produce an empty map.

Before coding, name the tests: empty input, repeated vehicle, multiple vehicles and a record without a vehicle. Decide how to handle a null list according to the agreed contract; you can reject it or return an empty result, but do not silently change the choice halfway through the exercise. These examples make your intended behaviour visible before syntax becomes a distraction.

In an isolated Apex test, create two synthetic Vehicle__c records and use their returned Id values in unsaved Test_Drive__c instances. Give vehicles a Name and any fields required by your own lab automation. Do not insert the appointments: their Vehicle__c values are enough for this input-only function. Test setup may perform DML; the method under review may not query or write. Place setup before Test.startTest(), then verify the result and that the method adds no SOQL or DML operations. Use real fixture Ids rather than guessing a custom-object Id prefix.

> **⚡ Try it**
>
> Explain the data structure and one sample by hand before opening the editor.

**Input-only contract and fixture; implement the Apex method yourself**

```text
Method: countByVehicle(List<Test_Drive__c> requests)
Result: Map<Id, Integer>
Input vehicle references: vehicleA.Id, vehicleA.Id, vehicleB.Id, null
Expected map: vehicleA.Id -> 2; vehicleB.Id -> 1
Expected empty input: empty map
No appointment query or DML is allowed inside the method.
State and test your choices for a null list and null list elements.
```

## 3. Review a flawed approach systematically

Imagine that a proposed solution queries all test drives for each vehicle inside a loop. It may return a correct count for a small example, yet it violates the stated input-only contract and repeats database work. A reviewer should first identify the mismatch in behaviour, then the scaling problem. “Queries in loops are bad” is less useful than explaining which unnecessary work occurs and how the input list already contains the needed information.

Review in a consistent order: requirement, correctness, security where applicable, resource use, maintainability and tests. Do not spend the first five minutes discussing variable names while a method counts the wrong records. In a real code review, distinguish a blocking issue from an optional improvement and give a concrete example that demonstrates the concern.



**Reusable working prompt or template**

```text
Review note example:
Blocking: this method queries records outside the supplied list.
Example: the input contains two requests for vehicle A, but the org has ten.
The required answer is 2; the database count can return 10.
Suggested direction: aggregate the supplied records in a Map<Id, Integer>.
Verification: test repeated vehicles, empty input and missing vehicle references.
```

## 4. Narrate decisions and recover from mistakes

While coding, explain changes in your approach rather than every keystroke. “I am using a map so that each record updates one count” is helpful. If you make a syntax error, read the message, isolate the issue and correct it. Calm diagnosis is part of the work; pretending the error did not happen makes the reasoning harder to follow.

Finish by running the agreed examples and reviewing the implementation against the contract. If time runs out, state what works, what remains and the next test you would run. Do not claim that unexecuted tests passed. For peer practice, alternate roles: one person implements the task and the other records clarifications, decisions and missed checks.

> **🔎 Check your understanding**
>
> The observer can explain your algorithm, one rejected alternative and the evidence that the result is correct.

## ⚡ Independent practice: Apply this to your own search

Implement the input-only count method, then review a deliberately flawed alternative.

1. Write the input and output contract, including null-list, null-element, and missing-vehicle handling.
2. Sketch the map algorithm with three records.
3. In a training org with the starter installed, implement the method and isolated Apex tests using test-created vehicle Ids and unsaved appointment inputs.
4. Review a query-per-record alternative and write two prioritised comments.
5. Record what you verified and what remains untested.

### Your work is ready when

- Counts use only the supplied list.
- Empty input and repeated vehicles are covered.
- The review identifies behaviour and scaling issues before style preferences.
- The report names the tested class, API version, actual test result and zero-query/zero-DML method check; unexecuted org checks are labelled outstanding.

## 🔎 Check your understanding

Answer in your own words before reading the explanations.

### 1. Why clarify whether database queries are allowed?

**Answer:** It defines the source of truth and prevents counting records outside the intended input.

### 2. What makes a review comment actionable?

**Answer:** It names the issue, explains its impact, gives an example and suggests a direction or verification.

### 3. What should you report if a test was not run?

**Answer:** Say it was not run and explain the intended check; do not present it as passing evidence.

## What to take away

- Clarify the contract.
- Test examples before polishing.
- Review impact before style.

## Sources and further reading

- [Apex Map methods](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/platform/apex-ref/references/apex-ref/apex-methods-system-map.html)
- [Apex unit tests and isolated test data](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/modules/apex_testing/apex_testing_intro)
- [Salesforce tech and product careers: interview context](https://www.salesforce.com/company/careers/teams/tech-and-product/)

# Discuss Your Experience and Evaluate the Employer

JR10 · jobs · About 60 minutes with practice

![Two drawn people in a calm professional conversation with three concrete project story cards](https://sam-portal-production.up.railway.app/lesson-images/JR10.jpg)

Prepare clear experience stories and questions that help you understand the work and team.

## Before you begin

- JR04: evidence-based experience accounts.
- One role description you want to discuss.

## What you will learn

- Explain decisions and learning.
- Ask about the actual work.
- Separate observations from assumptions.

## 1. Prepare stories with a real decision

Experience questions often ask about a difficult problem, feedback, collaboration or a mistake. Choose real events that you can discuss without exposing confidential information. A training or volunteer example can be useful when you label the context. The strongest story contains a decision you made, the information available at the time and what you learned from the result.

For example, you may have built a flow that worked for one test record but failed when several records were updated. Explain how you reproduced the failure, identified the repeated work, changed the design and checked the result. Do not replace the technical detail with “I worked hard and solved it.” The interviewer needs to understand how you approach uncertainty.

> **💡 Key idea**
>
> A useful story explains a decision and its consequences. It does not need to make you look flawless.

## 2. Keep responsibility and outcome clear

Use a short structure: situation, responsibility, action, result and reflection. Describe the situation only far enough to make the problem understandable. Spend most of the time on your actions and reasoning. If other people helped, identify their contribution. If the result was mixed, explain what improved and what remained unresolved.

Prepare three stories: one successful delivery, one diagnosis or mistake, and one response to feedback. For each, write a one-sentence lesson that changed how you work. “I now test a mixed batch before calling validation complete” is more concrete than “I learned to be careful.” Practise telling the story in two minutes, then allow follow-up questions.



## 3. Ask questions that reveal the actual work

An interview also gives you information about the role. Ask about the first projects, the balance between configuration and code, the release process, the development environments and how code review works. For a more senior role, ask who owns architectural decisions, incident response and technical debt prioritisation. Choose questions that matter to your target responsibilities rather than reading a long generic list.

Connect a question to something in the description. “You mention integration ownership. Does that include monitoring and handling failed messages, or mainly building new endpoints?” is more useful than assuming the phrase has one meaning. Ask for examples where possible. The answer may reveal a fit, a learning opportunity or a mismatch; record it without treating one imperfect answer as a complete judgment of the employer.

> **⚡ Try it**
>
> Prepare five questions, then choose the two or three that remain unanswered during the conversation.

## 4. Use the conversation to make a decision

After the interview, record what you learned about responsibilities, support, expectations and the next stage. Separate stated facts from your interpretation. “The team said developers rotate incident support” is a fact from the conversation. “The team is probably chaotic” is an interpretation that needs more evidence. If a practical condition is still unclear, note a follow-up question.

Review whether the role still matches the target from JR01. A role with a desirable title may involve work you do not want, while a differently titled role may offer the development you need. You do not need to decide everything during the call. The aim of your questions is to replace assumptions with information that supports a thoughtful decision.

> **🔎 Check your understanding**
>
> Your notes contain both evidence about your fit and unanswered questions about the role.

## ⚡ Independent practice: Apply this to your own search

Prepare three experience stories and a focused set of employer questions.

1. Choose real events with clear ownership.
2. Write the decision, result and reflection for each.
3. Practise a two-minute version and one technical follow-up.
4. Prepare questions linked to the actual role description.
5. After a mock conversation, separate facts, interpretations and open questions.

### Your work is ready when

- Stories contain a specific action and evidence, not only personal qualities.
- Team contributions and limitations remain accurate.
- Employer questions clarify responsibilities you care about.

## 🔎 Check your understanding

Answer in your own words before reading the explanations.

### 1. Should a mistake story hide the mistake?

**Answer:** No. Explain the response, verification and lesson while respecting confidentiality.

### 2. Why ask for an example of the release process?

**Answer:** An example reveals how the process works in practice and reduces ambiguity.

### 3. How should you record an uncertain impression?

**Answer:** Label it as an interpretation and identify what additional information would confirm or change it.

## What to take away

- Explain decisions and learning.
- Ask about the actual work.
- Separate observations from assumptions.

## Sources and further reading

- [How Salesforce hires](https://www.salesforce.com/company/careers/culture/how-we-hire/)

# Build a Preparation Plan from Interview Feedback

JR11 · jobs · About 60 minutes with practice

![An interview note becoming three focused practice steps and a follow-up calendar check](https://sam-portal-production.up.railway.app/lesson-images/JR11.jpg)

Turn an interview or mock session into a focused cycle of practice and reassessment.

## Before you begin

- One interview, mock session or self-check result to review.

## What you will learn

- Capture specific evidence.
- Choose a small number of relevant priorities.
- Reassess with changed conditions.

## 1. Capture evidence while it is fresh

After an interview, write down the questions you remember, the answers you gave and the moments where you were unsure. Do not claim to reconstruct every word. Label approximate notes as approximate and avoid copying confidential tasks or materials you are not authorised to retain. Your aim is to identify learning needs, not to build a leaked question bank.

Separate four kinds of issue: missing knowledge, weak application, unclear explanation and unfamiliar interview format. If you can define Queueable Apex but cannot explain retry behaviour, that is an application gap. If your answer is technically sound but takes five minutes to reach the point, the main improvement may be communication. Different gaps need different practice.

> **💡 Key idea**
>
> “I did badly” is not an actionable finding. Name the specific behaviour you want to improve.

## 2. Prioritise by relevance and evidence

Create a table with topic, observed difficulty, target-role relevance, practice task and verification. Choose two priorities for the next cycle. A repeated gap in the core responsibilities of your target role usually deserves attention before a niche topic that appeared once. Do not assume that a rejection was caused by the one answer you disliked; hiring decisions may include information you do not have.

For example, write: “Bulk Apex: I placed a query inside a loop during the exercise. Practice: aggregate input records and query related data once. Verification: run a test with 200 input records and explain the query count.” This is much clearer than “Review Apex.” It defines a behaviour, an action and evidence that the action helped.



## 3. Plan a small cycle with different activities

Use a short cycle that fits your schedule. One session can rebuild the concept from a primary source, another can implement a small example, and a third can explain a variation aloud. Leave time to review the result. Reading, coding and explaining expose different weaknesses; doing only one of them can create a misleading sense of progress.

A sample week might contain two forty-minute technical sessions and one twenty-minute mock discussion. Adjust the duration to your availability. The important part is completing a small loop: identify a gap, practise it, verify the result and reassess. Keep job applications moving at a realistic pace if you are actively searching; endless preparation can become a way to avoid testing your fit in the market.

> **⚡ Try it**
>
> Put one verification task on the calendar before adding more learning resources.

## 4. Reassess with a changed scenario

At the end of the cycle, solve a related problem with a different condition. If you practised a successful callout, try a timeout and duplicate response. If you studied sharing, explain what happens when a separate permission set grants a permission that is muted in a group. A changed scenario tests the mechanism rather than memorisation of a single answer.

Record the outcome as demonstrated, partly demonstrated or still unclear, with a short reason. Keep the original note so you can see what changed. If the topic remains unclear, reduce the scope and identify the missing prerequisite. If you can explain and demonstrate it, move to the next priority while scheduling occasional retrieval practice. The plan should evolve from evidence, not from a growing pile of bookmarks.

> **🔎 Check your understanding**
>
> A completed preparation task includes an observed result and a decision about what to do next.

## ⚡ Independent practice: Apply this to your own search

Turn one interview or mock session into a one-week preparation plan.

1. Record the questions and uncertainties you remember.
2. Classify each gap as knowledge, application, explanation or format.
3. Choose two priorities related to your target role.
4. Schedule learning, implementation and explanation tasks.
5. Test a changed scenario and record the result.

### Your work is ready when

- Each priority has a concrete practice task and a verification method.
- The plan fits the time you actually have.
- Reassessment uses a variation, not only the original question.

## 🔎 Check your understanding

Answer in your own words before reading the explanations.

### 1. Can you know why an employer rejected you without feedback?

**Answer:** Usually not. Record what you observed and avoid turning a guess into a confirmed cause.

### 2. Why classify the type of gap?

**Answer:** Because reading documentation will not by itself fix every application or communication problem.

### 3. When is a preparation task complete?

**Answer:** When you have performed the planned verification and recorded what it demonstrates and what remains unclear.

## What to take away

- Capture specific evidence.
- Choose a small number of relevant priorities.
- Reassess with changed conditions.

## Sources and further reading

- [Get job ready](https://trailhead.salesforce.com/content/learn/trails/get-job-ready)
